Four more ways to lose a draft

Every chapter holds the same two drawings: a pencil wireframe on paper and the finished design underneath. A WebGL2 shader gives each pixel a fuel value and decides, pixel by pixel, which drawing you see. Scrolling pushes the front through the fuel. Each element shapes that fuel differently and does different damage at the edge.

Water
A tide rises from the bottom of the page. Ahead of it the paper soaks up water: it greys, and the pencil bleeds. Below the waterline the finished design wavers through moving water, with caustic light drifting across it.
Air
Wind comes in from the left. The paper flutters before the gust arrives. Then the wind strips it off in streaks and carries it away. Each paper fleck keeps the colour of the bit of sketch it tore from.
Earth
The page splits into plates. Hairline cracks open along their edges first. Then each plate sinks into the dark, shedding pebbles and dust. The whole plate goes at once because its timing comes from its centre. Scroll fast and the ground shakes.
Lightning
Four bolts grow as branching random walks, and each one gets its own distance field. When your scroll reaches a bolt, it strikes. The bolt flashes and the reveal spreads out from the channel it burned.

Click or tap the paper in any chapter to drop a stone, send a gust, start a quake or call down a bolt. Scroll back up and the draft comes back.

Burn the draft More on the playground

Raw WebGL2 and canvas, no libraries. One WebGL canvas draws all four chapters, each with its own fragment shader. Without WebGL2 each chapter falls back to a wipe. With reduced motion on, they cross-fade, and the lightning chapter doesn't flash.
